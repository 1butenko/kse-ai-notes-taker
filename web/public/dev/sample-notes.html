Lecture 3: Supply, demand and market equilibrium

Principles of Economics · sample notes for design work

Learning goals Explain how buyers and sellers set a market price; find the equilibrium from linear demand and supply; predict how shocks move price and quantity.

1. Demand

The quantity demanded is how much buyers are willing and able to buy at a given price. Holding everything else fixed, a higher price lowers it: the law of demand. We write a linear demand curve as

Qd = a − b·P,   b > 0

Income, the prices of related goods, tastes, expectations and the number of buyers shift the whole curve; a change in the good's own price only moves us along it.

2. Supply

The quantity supplied rises with price, because higher prices cover the higher marginal cost of producing more:

Qs = c + d·P,   d > 0

3. Equilibrium

Setting Qd = Qs gives the equilibrium price and quantity:

P* = (a − c) / (b + d),    Q* = a − b·P*
Worked example With Qd = 100 − 2P and Qs = 10 + 4P: P* = 90 / 6 = 15 and Q* = 100 − 30 = 70.

4. Shifts and their effects

ShockCurvePriceQuantity
Incomes rise (normal good)Demand right↑↑
Input costs fallSupply right↓↑
Substitute gets cheaperDemand left↓↓
New tax on producersSupply left↑↓

When both curves shift at once, the direction of one variable is certain and the other depends on which shift is larger.

5. Elasticity, briefly

The price elasticity of demand measures how strongly quantity responds to price:

ε = (ΔQ / Q) ÷ (ΔP / P)

Demand is elastic when |ε| > 1, so revenue falls as price rises; inelastic when |ε| < 1. We return to this in Lecture 4.

Summary

  • Demand slopes down, supply slopes up; other factors shift the curves.
  • The market clears at the price where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
  • Compare the old and new equilibrium to read the effect of any shock.
